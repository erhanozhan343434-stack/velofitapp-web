How to Keep a Backup Vegetable Ready for Dinner

Keep one vegetable on hand for evenings when the produce drawer is empty. Choose something you enjoy, know how to prepare, and can pair with a meal you already make. Give it a specific job before you put it away.

Choose your backup

A bag of plain frozen broccoli or a can of no-salt-added carrots can be a useful starting point. The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services lists frozen vegetables without added butter or sauce and low-sodium or no-salt-added canned vegetables among its lower-sodium shopping options.

Pick a package size that fits your storage space and household. Read its preparation directions before buying, especially if you’ll rely on a microwave or a single stovetop burner.

Pair it with a familiar dinner

Write down one pairing. For example, serve frozen broccoli alongside pasta, or heat canned carrots as a side for a bean quesadilla. Prepare each item according to its package directions. These are meal ideas you can adapt to what you like and already have.

Keep the plan concrete: “When I make pasta and have no fresh vegetables, I’ll prepare the broccoli from the freezer.” Put that note where you keep your dinner list.

Try it on a busy evening

Suppose Wednesday’s dinner is pasta with tomato sauce. Before starting, check the broccoli directions and work its cooking time into your routine. Set out the equipment it needs so you can prepare it while the rest of dinner comes together. Serve the amount that works for your meal.

Afterward, note whether the pairing suited you and whether the preparation was manageable. If it needed equipment you rarely use, choose a different vegetable next time. A backup you’re comfortable preparing is easier to include in your actual dinner plans.

Add a replacement to your list

When you finish the package, add it to your shopping list. If you didn’t enjoy it, write down a different option to try instead. Keep following the package’s storage directions for anything left.

For shopping guidance, see HHS’s lower-sodium foods list, updated in April 2026.
