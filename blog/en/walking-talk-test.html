How to Use the Talk Test on a Walk

If you’re trying to tell whether a walk feels easy or brisk, try saying a sentence out loud while you walk. The CDC describes a simple rule of thumb for moderate intensity: you can still talk, but singing would be difficult. Use that cue to compare two stretches of the same walk.

Start with a route you know

Pick a short, familiar route, such as the sidewalk from your building to the corner and back. Start at a pace that feels easy. On a clear stretch where you can keep your attention on your surroundings, try saying, “I’m walking to the corner and heading back.” Notice whether saying it feels comfortable.

Compare two stretches

On the return trip, pick up the pace a little if that feels comfortable. Say the same sentence again. If speaking is still comfortable but singing would be hard, that fits the CDC’s moderate-intensity cue. If you can manage only a few words before pausing for breath, the CDC describes that as a cue for vigorous intensity. Slow down if you meant to keep the walk easy.

Write one note for next time

Record the route and where your breathing changed. For example: “To the corner and back. Talking felt easy on the flat stretch. On the uphill block I had to pause mid-sentence, so I slowed down.” If you want a similar walk next time, that note tells you where to ease up without watching a speed target.

For the full explanation of how breathing and conversation can help you gauge effort, see the CDC guide to measuring physical activity intensity.
