A Three-Dinner Grocery List for Busy Weeknights

If planning seven dinners feels like too much, start with three. Pick three nights you expect to cook, then build the grocery list around ingredients that can go into more than one meal. Here’s a sample week built around rice, bell peppers, plain yogurt, and a few pantry staples.

Check the kitchen before you shop

Look in the pantry, fridge, and freezer. If you already have rice, canned beans, and frozen peppers, build around them. Check your calendar next. Save the meal with the most chopping for a night you have more time, and give the busiest night the simplest dinner.

Three dinners using one grocery list

  1. Rice bowls with black beans and peppers. Serve cooked rice with warmed black beans, sautéed peppers, and plain yogurt mixed with lime. If you have frozen corn, add some to the peppers.
  2. Black bean tacos with cabbage. Fill warm tortillas with black beans and sliced peppers. Add shredded cabbage and a spoonful of the lime yogurt for crunch and tang.
  3. Chickpea and tomato skillet. Simmer chickpeas with diced tomatoes, peppers, and a pinch of cumin. Spoon the skillet over rice and serve extra cabbage on the side with lime.

Write the list by store section

  • Produce: bell peppers, a small cabbage, and limes. Bagged shredded cabbage works if you prefer less chopping.
  • Pantry: rice, black beans, chickpeas, canned diced tomatoes, tortillas, and cumin.
  • Refrigerated: plain yogurt.

Cross off what you already have and adjust the amounts for your household. If you won’t use the rest of a cabbage, buy a smaller bag of slaw mix or use lettuce you already have. If you have a different grain on hand, use it in the bowl and the skillet.

Keep one backup dinner

Plans change. If you get home late on skillet night, warm beans, tortillas, and frozen vegetables for a quick backup. Move the skillet to a night when you have time for it. After the third dinner, note which ingredients went unused so you can buy less or plan another use next week.
