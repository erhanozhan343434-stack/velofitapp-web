How to Keep a Simple Weekly Activity Log

A useful activity log can fit on a single page. Write down when you moved, what you did, how long you spent, and one practical detail you want to remember. Review those notes before making next week’s plan.

Use four fields

Start with the date and time. Add the activity and its duration. Finish with a short note about how it fit your day. “Tuesday, 12:30 p.m. — walked for 10 minutes — the route near work was convenient” gives you something concrete to plan around.

Record what happened even when it differs from your plan. If you planned a walk but stayed at your desk, write down the obstacle. A meeting that ran late suggests a different scheduling problem from forgetting your walking shoes.

Keep different activities visible

The CDC’s U.S. adult guidance includes both aerobic activity and muscle strengthening. Keep a separate line for each kind of activity so you can see your week clearly. The CDC also explains that aerobic activity can be spread across the week in smaller chunks. Log the time you actually spent, then use the CDC’s activity guide to understand the types of activity described in its recommendations.

Review one week before planning another

Suppose your notes show that two lunchtime walks happened, while an evening session kept getting pushed aside by errands. For the next week, pencil in those two lunch breaks again and choose a different time for the session you missed. This is a scheduling example you can adapt to your own routine.

End your review with one sentence: “Next week I’ll put my shoes by my work bag and reserve Tuesday’s lunch break.” Keep the log brief enough that you can fill it in after an activity. Add detail only when it helps you make your next decision.
