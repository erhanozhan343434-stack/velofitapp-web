How to Pack Tomorrow’s Lunch From Tonight’s Dinner

Pack tomorrow’s lunch while you’re serving tonight’s dinner. Set out a shallow container, portion what you want to take, and put it in the fridge promptly. You’ll have one less decision to make in the morning.

Decide what to pack before serving dinner

Pick a part of dinner you’d be happy to eat again. If you’re making sheet-pan chicken and vegetables, set aside a serving for lunch and pack a roll separately. If dinner is lentil soup, put a serving in a shallow, leakproof container and add crackers to your bag in the morning. Set aside only what you expect to eat.

Chill it and write the date

FoodSafety.gov recommends cooling cooked leftovers promptly in shallow containers. Refrigerate perishable food within two hours, or within one hour if it’s above 90°F where the food is sitting. Write the date you cooked it on the lid. Plan to use refrigerated cooked leftovers within four days or freeze portions you won’t use in that time.

Make the morning handoff easy

Put the container on a fridge shelf where you’ll notice it and leave your lunch bag by your keys. For example, on Monday night you might pack Tuesday’s chicken and vegetables, then place the roll and a napkin in the bag. In the morning, grab the container just before leaving and keep it cold until lunch. If you reheat the leftovers, the federal guidance recommends 165°F.

For cooling, storage, and reheating guidance, see FoodSafety.gov’s leftover storage section.
