How to Read Serving Size on a Nutrition Label

If the Nutrition Facts label says 230 mg of sodium, start by checking what that number covers. It usually applies to one labeled serving. The bag, box, or bottle may hold several servings.

Read the two lines at the top

Serving size tells you the amount used for the numbers below. A bag of pretzels might list its serving in pieces and grams. Servings per container tells you how many of those servings the package holds. The FDA bases the labeled serving size on what people typically consume at one time. You can use it as a reference for the amount you actually eat.

Try the math with a bag of pretzels

Suppose an example label lists three servings in the bag, 12 pretzels per serving, and 230 mg of sodium per serving. If you put 18 pretzels in your lunch bag, divide 18 by 12 to get 1.5 servings. Multiply 230 mg by 1.5 and you get 345 mg of sodium for the amount you packed. Use the numbers on your own package when you try it.

Check for a per-package column

Some labels show one column per serving and another for the entire package. If you’re using the whole package, read the per-package column directly. For part of a package, work from the per-serving column and multiply by the number of servings you’re using.

See the FDA’s serving size guide for a labeled example and the FDA’s guide to the full Nutrition Facts label for more on reading the two columns.
